Glossary series · Part 1

Source of truth.

The one system that owns a specific type of data — the version everyone trusts.

Why it matters: If two systems both claim to own stock quantities, you don't have a source of truth. You have a conflict.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 2

Idempotency.

A property where repeating the same operation doesn't create duplicates.

Why it matters: It's how you stop the same invoice from being imported twice and paid twice.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 3

Audit trail.

A record of who did what, when, and (where possible) what changed.

Why it matters: It's how you resolve disputes without relying on memory or chat history.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 4

Segregation of duties.

Separating the person who creates a transaction from the person who approves it.

Why it matters: It catches honest mistakes and makes unauthorized changes much harder.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 5

Draft-for-approval.

A workflow where a system prepares a transaction but a human must approve it before it takes effect.

Why it matters: It's how AI can speed up work without taking responsibility away from your team.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 6

Write-back.

Sending a confirmed record from one system to another.

Why it matters: It's how an integration stays in sync — and why source-of-truth rules matter before enabling it.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 7

Tenant isolation.

Ensuring one company's data is completely separated from another's.

Why it matters: It's a baseline security requirement for any multi-company platform.

stacko
Glossary series · Part 8

Validation warning.

A flag raised when data is missing, uncertain, or inconsistent — before it becomes a problem.

Why it matters: It's how systems avoid silent errors.
